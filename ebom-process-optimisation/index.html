01 · Business context

The challenge

Each EBOM revision could trigger additional work and cost when errors or validation issues were found during the hand-offs between departments.

After the customer drawing was confirmed, the EBOM was created and sent to Production for technical validation. Once accepted, it moved towards Procurement. Before that hand-off, supporting documentation and technical specification sheets had to be uploaded by System Engineering, followed by validation involving Software, Hardware and PLM.

Failures could arise from human mistakes or system issues. A rejected EBOM required another version to be created, adding process cost and putting pressure on the annual budget.

Project objective: reduce process errors and avoid unnecessary EBOM version creation. The stated target was a 20% reduction in failures / failure rate; confirm the exact KPI definition before publishing this as a formal target.
02 · Process understanding

Where rework entered the workflow

The process required coordinated inputs and validation across customer-facing, engineering and operational teams.

Customer drawing→Customer confirmation→EBOM creation→Production validation→System Engineering documents→Software / Hardware / PLM validation→Procurement

A rejection or missing / incorrect input could send the EBOM back for correction and a new version. The workflow above reflects the sequence described for this project; individual company-specific approval rules are not reproduced.

03 · Analysis & intervention

How the improvement was approached

A structured problem-solving process was used to move from broad symptoms to actionable causes and department-level measures.

Understand the problem

Facilitated brainstorming sessions to define the business problem, the process impact and the main failure patterns.

Gather perspectives

Conducted structured interviews with relevant stakeholders and department heads to examine different explanations.

Identify root causes

Used a fishbone (Ishikawa) analysis to organise potential causes, including human and system-related errors.

Agree preventive actions

Discussed causes with department heads and planned measures intended to prevent repeat failures and avoid unnecessary revisions.

Controlling contribution: translated the operational issue into a concise management report, with a clear storyline and standard KPI definitions so stakeholders could understand the situation quickly.

06 · Outcome

Reported business impact

  • 22% improvement reported over eight months. The original project notes describe this as “22% failure rate”; this page interprets it as a 22% reduction in failures because the stated goal was to reduce failures. Please verify whether the result is a relative reduction, a percentage-point change, or a final failure rate.
  • €3 million reported savings. This is presented as the outcome supplied in the project notes; the measurement period, calculation method and attribution should be confirmed before public use.
  • Improved process transparency. The dashboard enabled more precise tracking of affected CRAs by project, customer, product family, time period and reason category.
  • More structured problem-solving. Fishbone analysis and stakeholder discussions helped organise failure causes and define preventive measures with the relevant departments.
